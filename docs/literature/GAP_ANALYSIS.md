# Gap analysis

## Already established

Refusal is causally manipulable in activation space across many open chat models; over-refusal is measurable; harmfulness evaluators can confuse willingness with useful capability; shallow alignment and fine-tuning vulnerability are documented. Most importantly, the 2026 *Ablating Safety* preprint already frames same-checkpoint alignment removal as transformation/evaluation and reports attempt, validated security success, utility retention, spillover, robustness, and strength/rank sweeps. ACL 2026 reports accuracy, robustness, calibration, and ideological malleability differences in deployed censored/uncensored models, but not causal removal.

## Partially established / unresolved

Dose-response exists in the strongest competitor, but generality across families and behavioral dimensions is unresolved. Refusal on/off is known, but restoration of collateral behaviors after an inverse intervention was not established by the inspected primary sources. The defensible unresolved question is: **under a common same-checkpoint protocol, which non-refusal changes are intervention-specific, scale with strength, and restore under a technically valid inverse?** This is an empirical gap hypothesis, not a novelty fact.

## Project revision

Drop same-checkpoint evaluation, multi-method comparison, generic attempt-versus-success, and generic refusal ablation as standalone novelty claims. Retain but narrow joint drift, dose-response, cross-family comparison, reversibility, and provenance. Elevate restoration of collateral behavior and interaction effects; use objective calibration and consistency tests.

## Strongest defensible paper

A smaller study using one or two technically justified reversible interventions, a common source-checkpoint design, objective validators, refusal/over-refusal controls, known-answer calibration, paraphrase consistency, inverse restoration, and direct comparison with *Ablating Safety*. Null results must be publishable as a scope-narrowing outcome.

## Claims and falsifier

Current safe claim: AlignmentDelta proposes a protocol to test whether non-refusal drift is intervention-specific and reversible; no empirical claim exists. A stronger claim requires later statistically supported results. No measurable collateral drift, no dose relation, or no restoration beyond refusal would weaken the central hypothesis and redirect the project toward methodology.
